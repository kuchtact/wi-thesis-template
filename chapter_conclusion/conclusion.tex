In this thesis we present a number of diagnostic upgrades to the Terrestrial Reconnection EXperiment that accelerate data taking, increase resolution, and allow us to measure new plasma parameters that were previously unattainable.

\subsection{Pressure Waves to Measure Density}

In chapter \ref{sch:pressure_wave} we found that a pressure wave is generated by the initialization of a reverse field required for magnetic reconnection. By measuring the speed of this wave as it propagated radially inwards, which was found to be at approximately the Alfv\'en speed, we can derive the initial radial plasma density profile for every shot. The results of this method matched well with Langmuir probe measurements and can be used for studies of cross-field diffusion and for identifying outlier shots.

\subsection{Advancing Langmuir Probe Analysis}

Chapter \ref{sch:multitip_probe} focused on improving our understanding of a multi-tip Langmuir probe for measuring the full IV-characteristic at each time point. By implementing a number of methods, from improved plasma theories to fitting routines, we were able to measure far lower densities with higher noise resilience and greater accuracy.

\subsection{Measurements of Pressure Anisotropy during Laboratory Reconnection}

In the last chapter we discussed the development of a novel diagnostic for measuring pressure anisotropy consisting of twelve directional Langmuir probes. We developed two methods for extracting plasma parameters from the raw measurements, one using integrals of the distribution function and the other via spherical harmonics of the measurements. We found that our three coil system was sufficiently collisionless to allow for the generation of pressure anisotropy. With a low guide field, the perpendicular pressure was higher than the parallel pressure in the outflow whereas with a sufficiently high guide field, the anisotropy approached the marginal firehose condition near the location of a current jet that was observed to bisect the outflow.
